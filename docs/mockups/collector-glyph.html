Collector glyph — exploration

Throwaway. Explores replacing the circle-and-impeller collector with a body that carries its own name and its own plug, the way a machine already does. Fold the outcome into docs/mockups/canvas.html and archive this.

What is wrong with the circle

Two things, and they are the same thing. The collector's name floats outside the glyph — every other named piece on the canvas now carries its name inside its body — and the collector's plug is invisible: a system's blower has a plug exactly like a tool does, and today the only sign of it is the amber setup dot. The impeller spiral is what is eating the room both need. It buys nothing a shape cannot say on its own.

Hard ceiling: 76 × 76. The router inflates every device footprint by CLEARANCE = 15 before treating it as an obstacle, and the A* lattice is half a cell = 54. A half-extent of 38 inflates to 53 and leaves the lattice line on the cell boundary usable; 39 blocks it, and a duct can no longer pass along the row or column beside the collector. So the barrel gets 76 wide by 76 tall at the most — 8 units taller than a machine's 76 × 68, no more. All differentiation has to come out of the silhouette, not the size.

The three shapes

Same two rows in all three — name on top, plug chip beneath it, both borrowed whole from the machine body so nothing new has to be invented for them. Only the outline differs. Each is drawn beside a machine and a gate at 1:1, because the question is not whether the shape is nice, it is whether it is telling apart.

A · Cyclone

A real cyclone. The cone is unmistakable and no other piece on the canvas has a sloping edge — but the taper is exactly where the plug chip wants to be, so the chip has to ride high and the cone stays empty.

B · Barrel

A drum seen head-on: fat corners, a lid arc across the top and a base band at the bottom. Full 76-wide interior for both rows. Against a machine's rx 11 it is the same family, one size rounder — which is either the point or the problem.

C · Bag house

Domed top, flat base — the single-stage bagger most of these shops actually own. The dome reads at a glance and, unlike the cone, it gives the rows back their room. Its arc is also where the trunk lands, which the cone and the barrel both handle more gracefully.

Where the duct lands

Today a run may arrive at the circle from any side and it looks the same from all of them. A box has edges, and the shape has to survive every one — the collector usually sits top-left, so right and bottom are the common cases, but nothing stops a shop that feeds it from above.

B, fed three ways

Left: fed from the right, the everyday case. Middle: running — the plug row goes green and says what it is drawing, exactly as a tool's does. Right: no plug yet, so it keeps the amber setup dot on its corner.

What this decides

QuestionAnswer here
Impeller spiralGone. It was the only thing in the circle and it said less than the outline can.
The nameInside the body, same .glabel, same trim rule as a machine's — so a long name can no longer sit over a duct.
The plugDrawn, as the same dock chip a machine wears. It is the collector's plug, and it draws under the collector's name.
Footprint76 × 76 — halfW/halfH go 30 → 38. Both are read by the router, so this is the number that matters.
ColourIts own, not the machine teal. A collector is not a machine and the shape alone is a thin thing to hang that on.

Still open
