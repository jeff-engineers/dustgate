Exploration · 2026-08-20

Where does the hood go when its run arrives from the side?

A pickup can only be entered from directly above today, so an aux run fed from a collector below it has to climb over the whole shop to get on top. Letting a hood take a side entry fixes the route — but it has to keep satisfying the rule that a duct always lands on a glyph, and that glyphs prefer the top edge.

What it does now

Measured, not guessed: the run leaves DC2 from its right outlet — a collector has no top outlet at all — then climbs to the top of the board because the hood's only inlet faces down.

The two options

A · The hood follows its duct

like the spigot

The hood rotates onto whichever edge its run actually arrives at, exactly the way the square spigot already does. Top stays preferred; the side is what you get when the side run is genuinely shorter.

  • One rule, not two. Both glyphs obey "sit where your duct lands."
  • Shortest possible pipe — straight up from the collector, no hook.
  • A tapered hood pointing sideways is a slightly odd picture of an overarm guard, which really does hang above the work.
  • Two pickups on the same machine can now sit on different edges — more places to keep straight.

B · The hood stays on top

pipe hooks over

The hood never leaves the top edge. The run climbs the right side, crosses just above the box, and drops in — a short hook past one corner, not a lap of the shop.

  • A hood is always a hood, one shape in one place, on every machine.
  • Matches the thing it draws: an overarm pickup is above the work.
  • Costs one extra corner and a stretch of pipe running along the top of the box — close to the machine's own name.
  • Two hoods on one top edge start to crowd the spigot already sitting there.

Either way the loop dies. The bug is that a hood has exactly one inlet and it faces down; both options give it more. The choice is only what the drawing says.

What I'd pick: B. The top-edge rule is the one you've had to repeat most, and it is the one that makes a drawing readable without tracing pipe. A hook past one corner is cheap; a glyph that moves to a different edge on every machine is a second rule to hold in your head. A is the better answer only if you'd rather the picture never carry a pipe segment across a machine's own box.
